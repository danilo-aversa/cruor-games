## Source and copyright boundary

The Source Anchor cites Stephen King's official works page for *The Mist*, which
identifies the novella, its 1980 release, a community enclosed by strange mist,
concealed creatures, and the strain placed on trapped people.

The source contribution is restricted to high-level design principles:

- enclosure by an opaque environment;
- danger beyond reliable sight;
- shelter versus exposure;
- thresholds as consequential decisions;
- fear-driven social fracture.

## Dark Places identity

The authored location is **The White Refuge**. Before the enclosure, it was a
roadside refuge and settlement whose painted signs, bell codes, window lamps,
and named doorways made travel legible in bad weather. After the mist arrived,
familiar distances stopped agreeing with footsteps and copied accounts began to
change the relationship between landmarks.

The horror truth does not secretly rewrite the map. The mist learns how people
expect places and people to connect, then overlays false distance, copied
voices, anticipated silhouettes, and borrowed memories. Stable routes remain
possible through shared observation and physical evidence.

This distinction is essential: uncertainty affects perception and decision, not
the integrity of the underlying map.

## Orientation Drift

Orientation Drift is a state from 0 to 4. It advances only after listed events
and at most once per combat round or once per ten-minute exploration turn.
Triggers include crossing an unanchored threshold, following an unverified
voice or silhouette, losing mutual sight, and leaving a breach unsealed.

At Drift 2 or higher, failed navigation does not teleport the group or invalidate
its map:

- during exploration, the group returns to its last stable landmark and loses
  ten minutes;
- during combat, movement toward an unanchored exit ends at the last clearly
  seen landmark and remaining movement is lost.

Three counterplays reduce Drift:

1. name both landmarks and leave a visible route marker;
2. have two observers independently confirm the same threshold;
3. seal the announced breach.

At Drift 3, the GM presents a copied route, voice, or silhouette and states one
physical discrepancy before the players choose it.

At Drift 4, **White Breach** begins. The GM names the exact unanchored threshold
and announces one full combat round or ten-minute exploration turn of
counterplay. If the countdown completes, mist enters through that opening and
activates the selected hazard or encounter pressure. The anchored route to the
last stable refuge remains available.

## Recurring signs

Four recurring signs replace repeated generic fog scares with progressive
information:

1. **The White Edge Advances** establishes that watched and physically marked
   thresholds remain reliable.
2. **The Figure Someone Expected** reveals that resemblance is built from an
   observer's expectation and fails on landmark relationships.
3. **Borrowed Memory Arrives Late** separates copied shape, movement, and memory
   from physical evidence.
4. **The Window Writes a Warning** reconstructs a route protocol that works only
   when paired with visible landmarks.

Each sign has at least three variations, an interaction trigger, a useful
effect, counterplay, and a separate revelation link. Repetition therefore
changes what the players know instead of merely restating that the mist is
strange.

## Sensory and read-aloud policy

The sensory profile uses white boundaries, flattened echoes, internal dampness,
cold iron, returned breaths, and small directional contradictions. Every strong
cue preserves an actionable difference between stable evidence and copied
perception.

The read-aloud profile supplies original fragments for route boards, sealed
parlors, paired watch chairs, bell-code landings, witness records, handprints,
condensation script, altered markers, and announced breaches. It contains no
source quotations or source-specific names.

The repetition policy forbids exact text reuse across rooms, applies a one-room
sense cooldown, and does not permit automatic signature repetition.

## Session structure

The opening presents a refuge held together by paired marks, witness slates, and
a white-flamed route lantern. A handprint appears inside sealed glass while an
outside voice offers a private fact but associates it with the wrong room.

The session asks the group to:

- establish an anchored route;
- determine whether the caller is a person, copied memory, or lure;
- reconstruct the route protocol;
- decide which threshold, if any, may be opened.

The clue flow requires three revelations: stable route, borrowed memory, and
route protocol. Fallback clues expose evidence without resolving the players'
conclusion. Stall moves always include a discrepancy or route cue; they never
advance uncertainty by withholding all information.
