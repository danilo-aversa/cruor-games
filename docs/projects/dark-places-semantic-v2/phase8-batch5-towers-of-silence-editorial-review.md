## Source boundary

The dossier uses a deliberately bounded historical frame:

- Encyclopaedia Iranica describes Zoroastrian funerary rules intended to avoid
  contaminating earth, fire, or water with a corpse;
- later `daxma` usage includes roofless or open exposure structures, while
  practice varied across time and place;
- later handling could include collecting or containing bones after exposure;
- architectural boundaries, drainage, open courts, and receptacles can inform
  spatial design without copying sacred procedures;
- exposed remains are people, not environmental decoration;
- scavenger behavior must never be framed as proof of moral worth, purity, or
  spiritual success.

Primary editorial reference:

- Encyclopaedia Iranica, *Burial iii. In Zoroastrianism*:
  `https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/burial-iii/`

Supporting architectural context reviewed during drafting:

- Encyclopaedia Iranica, *Astodan*;
- UNESCO Tentative List, *Chilpik Dakhma*.

The Open Reliquary, refused passage, supernatural shadows, Sky Measure, moving
bones, and all game procedures are original Cruor fantasy. The candidate never
asks players to reproduce a prayer, purity formula, or living ritual as a game
solution.

## Dark Places editorial model

Candidate 1 contains the seven required semantic profiles:

1. **Place Identity — The Open Reliquary:** an elevated funerary complex whose
   interrupted material sequence has become a supernatural measuring system;
2. **Site Atmosphere — Sun, Stone, and Unfinished Passage:** hot lime, mineral
   air, dry wing sounds, warm boundary dust, and a cool sheltered retreat;
3. **Global Rule — Sky Measure:** a visible 0–4 track with separate combat and
   ten-minute exploration cadence;
4. **Recurring Signs:** sun rings, birdless shadows, scratch paths, and broken
   boundary warnings;
5. **Sensory Profile:** all seven sensory channels with open/sheltered spatial
   contrast;
6. **Read-Aloud Profile:** bounded fragments that foreground architecture,
   evidence, and route choice rather than spectacle;
7. **Session Guide:** three required revelations, three stall moves, a default
   route, and an announced final descent.

Sky Measure advances only from named disturbances and at most once per combat
round or ten-minute exploration turn. At Measure 3 the GM names the route at
risk; Measure 4 resolves only after one full step of counterplay. A sheltered
retreat remains visible. The procedure cannot create unseen creatures, silently
close every exit, rewrite the real map, or require ritual imitation.
