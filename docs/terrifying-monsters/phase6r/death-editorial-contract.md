# Terrifying Monsters — Death Effect Editorial Contract

## Purpose

A Death Effect is not decorative death prose and is not permission to punish the party after every successful kill. It defines a final, visible encounter event whose position, trigger, scope, and answers were already legible before the monster reached 0 Hit Points.

## Required structure

Every published Death Effect must provide:

1. a stable Graft ID and source anchor;
2. an explicit death trigger;
3. one or two authored aftermath abilities;
4. a bounded tactical, terrain, investigative, object, or action-economy consequence;
5. a visible telegraph before the killing blow;
6. a positioning answer;
7. a finite break or cleanup condition;
8. a non-damage interaction;
9. verified structured-rule and renderer parity.

## Family roles

Death Effects may occupy one or more of these roles:

- **Burst:** an immediate save or damage event whose radius and target set are explicit.
- **Persistent zone:** terrain or a hazard anchored to the corpse, with a concrete cleanup procedure.
- **Final escalation:** a bounded summon or state change created by surviving encounter resources.
- **Death snare:** a finite condition with escape and object statistics.
- **Evidence:** a precise, actionable clue or trace with a defined interaction procedure.

## Bundle policy

A second ability is allowed only when the death aftermath contains a separate procedure that deserves its own stat-block entry. It must not be used to split one sentence into multiple headings.

Approved bundles in this pass:

- `spectral-dust-death`: immediate revealing dust plus later grave-trace investigation;
- `silk-cocoon-remains`: immediate death snare plus cocooned evidence object.

## Scaling policy

CR scaling may alter radius, target count, or bounded summon count. It must not add hidden subrules or turn a manageable aftermath into an unbounded second encounter.

Scaled IDs:

- `toxic-detonation`;
- `purge-fluid-flood`;
- `egg-hatch-death`;
- `face-curse`.

## Prohibited legacy patterns

- random rolls applied to every remaining egg;
- generic “one clue tied to the Source Anchor” language;
- non-telegraphed death bursts;
- conditions whose duration is accidentally written as the lifetime of the terrain;
- undefined requirements such as “the body was burned” without timing or object scope;
- summons with no cap, placement, duration, initiative, or prevention method.
