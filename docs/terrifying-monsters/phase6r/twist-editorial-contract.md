# Terrifying Monsters — Twist Graft Editorial Contract

## Purpose

A Twist Graft changes the encounter after the players believe they understand its normal rules. It is not a passive numerical bonus and it is not simply another attack, movement option, body trait, horror reveal, weakness, or death effect.

A publishable Twist must create at least one of these changes:

- a visible phase transition;
- a countdown or pressure clock;
- a false premise that can be investigated before it breaks;
- a player-triggered retaliation whose timing can be controlled;
- a one-time terrain reconfiguration;
- a recognizable canonical feature that changes how the scene is approached;
- a resource conversion that creates a bounded second state.

## Required structure

Every Twist Graft must define:

1. **Trigger or premise.** The event, resource, condition, or scene assumption that activates the Twist.
2. **Visible change.** The GM and players can tell that the encounter has entered a new state.
3. **Bounded duration.** The change ends, can be broken, or is limited to a clear number of uses.
4. **Positioning answer.** Movement, spacing, terrain, line of sight, or target choice matters.
5. **Break condition.** The new state can be ended, prevented, redirected, or suppressed.
6. **Non-damage answer.** At least one meaningful response does not require reducing hit points.
7. **GM procedure.** The routine explains when to announce, apply, and remove the state.

## Forbidden patterns

A Twist is not publishable when it relies on:

- hidden d6 escalation rolls;
- random self-destruction without a visible warning window;
- automatic critical hits against surprised creatures;
- permanent or until-dawn combat stacking;
- unlimited bonuses from consuming corpses or objects;
- a passive damage tax that does not change decisions;
- a duplicate of the Body, Mind, Movement, Horror, Attack, Weakness, Death, or Lair slot;
- a phase change chosen secretly by the GM without a visible tell;
- a dependency on another optional Graft without a self-contained fallback.

## Slot boundaries

| Slot | Primary question |
|---|---|
| Body | What physical chassis or anatomy does the monster have? |
| Mind | Why does it choose this target or objective? |
| Movement | Which route does it use to change position? |
| Attack Pattern | What does it normally do offensively? |
| Horror | What sensory reveal makes the encounter frightening? |
| Twist | What changes after the normal encounter loop has been established? |
| Weakness | How can players deliberately exploit it? |
| Death | What happens after the monster dies? |
| Lair | How does the environment act independently of the monster? |

## Canonical names

When a Twist remains recognizably based on a Bestiary feature, preserve the canonical name. This review therefore keeps:

- **Undead Fortitude**;
- **Siege Monster**.

The stable internal IDs remain unchanged even when the published title is corrected.

## CR scaling

Scale only the part that must grow with encounter tier:

- affected radius;
- push distance;
- temporary hit points;
- number of web anchors or walls;
- size of a destruction hazard.

Do not scale by adding more independent branches, hidden rolls, or longer bookkeeping.

## Publication gate

The Twist family is publishable only when every Graft:

- passes Graft schema validation;
- compiles at CR 1, 5, 10, and 15;
- has verified structured-rule rendering parity;
- satisfies all four counterplay channels;
- has an explicit editorial decision and rationale;
- preserves stable IDs and required source anchors;
- passes the global Content Studio, registry, and public-hardening boundaries.
